Checklist

Documents every family should keep a digital copy of

A practical checklist, grouped the way families actually need things, plus tips for storing the copies safely.

By the Stashify teamUpdated 12 October 20264 min read

When something goes wrong (a lost wallet, a hospital admission, a flooded cupboard, a sudden visa appointment) the families who cope best are the ones who can pull up the right document in a minute. A digital copy does not replace an original, but it lets you prove, apply, claim and replace things far faster.

Use this checklist to gather digital copies for everyone in your household. Tick items off as you go, and skip anything that does not apply to you.

A digital copy is for reference and convenience. Some offices accept only originals, certified copies or official digital versions, so always check what the organisation in question asks for.

Identity and personal records

  • National ID cards for each family member (in India: Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID)
  • Passports: the photo page and the address page
  • Driving licences, front and back
  • Birth certificates
  • Marriage certificate
  • Recent passport-size photos (a clean digital photo saves a trip to the studio)
  • Caste, domicile or income certificates, if your family uses them for applications

Money and tax

  • Bank account details and a cancelled cheque for each main account
  • Recent bank statements, especially those used for loans or visas
  • Income tax returns and acknowledgements for the last few years
  • Salary slips and Form 16 (or your country's equivalent) for working members
  • Investment statements: mutual funds, fixed deposits, provident fund, shares
  • Loan agreements, sanction letters and repayment schedules
  • Credit card statements you may need to dispute

Home and property

  • Sale deed or rent agreement
  • Property tax receipts
  • Society share certificate or allotment letter, if applicable
  • Electricity, gas, water and internet bills (often used as address proof)
  • Warranty cards and invoices for major appliances and electronics
  • Home insurance policy

Health

  • Health insurance policy documents and the e-card for each member
  • Prescriptions for regular medicines
  • Recent test reports and discharge summaries
  • Vaccination records, especially for children and older parents
  • A short note of allergies, blood groups and doctors' contacts

Health papers are the ones people most often need in a hurry and in someone else's hands. Make sure at least two adults in the family can reach them.

Education and work

  • School and college mark sheets and passing certificates
  • Degree certificates and transcripts
  • Admission and fee receipts for current students
  • Offer letters, appointment letters and experience letters
  • Professional registrations and licences

Vehicles

  • Registration certificate
  • Motor insurance policy
  • Pollution under control certificate
  • Loan and hypothecation papers, if any
  • Service history and major repair invoices

Legal and long-term

  • Wills and nominations
  • Life insurance policies, with nominee details
  • Power of attorney documents
  • Pension papers for retired members
  • Affidavits and court papers, if any

Travel

  • Current visas and residence permits
  • Travel insurance for upcoming trips
  • Bookings and itineraries (move them out of email so you can find them offline)

How to store the copies safely

Gathering the documents is half the job. How you store them decides whether they help or hurt you.

  1. Scan, do not just photograph. A flat, clean PDF is easier to read and share. See the best way to scan documents with your phone.
  2. Group by family member or by topic, whichever matches how you look for things. Many families use topic sections with the person's name in each file name.
  3. Lock identity and money documents behind a fingerprint or PIN.
  4. Write down expiry dates and set reminders, especially for passports, insurance and licences. Our renewals guide explains how far ahead to set them.
  5. Keep a backup somewhere other than your phone, and make sure a trusted family member knows how to reach it in an emergency.
  6. Review once a year. Replace old statements, remove expired documents and add anything new.

Be careful about sharing whole documents in chats. When someone only needs to see that a document exists, send the relevant page rather than the full file.
